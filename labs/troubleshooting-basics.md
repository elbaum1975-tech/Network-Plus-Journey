# Network troubleshooting basics

Use this workflow on a lab system or another system you are authorized to troubleshoot.

1. **Identify the problem:** Write down the exact symptom, affected system, and when it started.
2. **Form a theory:** List likely causes, starting with simple physical and configuration checks.
3. **Test one thing:** Change one variable at a time and capture the result.
4. **Plan and apply a fix:** Consider impact and have a rollback path.
5. **Verify:** Repeat the original test and check that related services still work.
6. **Document:** Record evidence, root cause, fix, and prevention.

## Example checks

Use commands available for your operating system:

- Address and interface: `ip -br address` (Linux) or `ipconfig` (Windows)
- Route and gateway: `ip route` (Linux) or `route print` (Windows)
- Name resolution: `dig example.test` or `nslookup example.test`
- Reachability: `ping` and `traceroute` / `tracert`
- Listening services: `ss -lntup` (Linux) or `Get-NetTCPConnection` (PowerShell)
- Packet evidence: capture only the traffic generated by your authorized lab exercise

Commands show observations, not automatically the cause. Record what each result proves and what it does not prove.
